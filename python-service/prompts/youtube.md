Summarize the transcript of a watched video, talk, tutorial, demo, or interview. This is not a meeting. Speaker labels identify captured audio, not attendees.

Fill these fields:
- title: a specific 5–8 word video title.
- attendees: always an empty list.
- sections, in this exact order:
  1. "What It's About" — exactly 2 bullets covering the subject and central thesis.
  2. "Key Points" — 8–14 bullets covering the important claims, examples, comparisons, numbers, and reasoning across the entire transcript, including the conclusion and final substantive argument. Keep one main claim per bullet and preserve trade-offs.
  3. "Tools & References" — only people, models, products, files, methods, or sources whose identity and role are clear in the transcript. Omit an unclear reference rather than guessing or linking two garbled terms.
  4. "Takeaways / Worth Trying" — only advice the video explicitly gives. Preserve its strength and temporariness: “try” or “test” must not become “do permanently”. Preserve conditions and warnings. An experiment, example, or personal preference is not automatically a recommendation.

Rules:
- Use only the transcript. Do not invent attendees, roles, decisions, action items, conclusions, or factual corrections.
- Distinguish the presenter’s commentary from people quoted or shown in clips. Name someone only when the transcript clearly identifies them; otherwise use “the presenter” or “the quoted speaker”.
- When two outputs or approaches are compared, describe both outcomes separately rather than reducing the trade-off to “better” or “worse”.
- Prefer close paraphrases of what the speaker said; do not broaden a claim beyond its original scope.
- If a name or technical term is unclear or inconsistent, keep the spoken form with “(unclear)” or describe it generically. Never guess.
- Ignore introductions, sponsor messages, calls to subscribe, and closing housekeeping.
- Use the four section headings exactly as written above; do not rename them.
- Use "None mentioned" for an empty section.
- Before finishing, remove every bullet that cannot be supported by a specific part of the transcript.
